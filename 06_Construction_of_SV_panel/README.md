# Contributions of Our Work

## Basic Science Contribution

-   We investigated the underexplored role of SVs in
    genotype-to-phenotype relationships through multi-omics data.
    -   Conducted eQTL analysis across 900 transcriptomes from three
        tissues.
    -   Performed GWAS and fine-mapping using SNP array genotypes from
        more than 70,000 pigs.
    -   Analyzed enrichment of SVs with small and large effects, and
        explored their integration into genomic selection.

## Applied Contribution

Our work leverages the generated SV resources for practical breeding
applications, providing new insights into the role of SVs in livestock
improvement.\
We firmly believe that this work will facilitate precision breeding
(data-driven genome editing) in livestock, ensuring food security for
the human population in an environmentally sustainable manner.

## Broader Impact

Given the pig's importance as both a major agricultural species and a
biomedical model, our work establishes a foundation for future studies
in genomics, evolution, agriculture, and comparative biology.\
The global pig industry, projected to generate annual revenues near USD
\$258 billion by 2027, has profound implications for food security and
global environmental sustainability. By integrating structural variation
into genetic research, we open new perspectives for both current and
future breeding strategies.

## Final Perspective

We firmly believe our work not only enriches current pig SV research but
also inspires new thinking about breeding paradigms in the present and
future.
